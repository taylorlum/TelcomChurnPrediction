This project centers around remedying the rising churn rates at Telcom Inc. by shifting the company towards data-driven, proactive retention strategies. Currently, Telcom is facing losses in recurring revenue because customer dissatisfaction is not recognized until it’s too late. With the goal of maximizing customer lifetime value, this approach uses predictive modeling to identify high-risk accounts before they churn as well as descriptive analytics to understand why customers leave and develop strategies to address these factors. By providing an end-to-end analytics solution, this project delivers clear benefits to key stakeholders. The CFO gains justification to reallocate investment from customer acquisition to retention that’s backed by data, customer service and marketing teams gain actionable tools for proactive engagement, and high-risk customers benefit from an improved service experience and tailored incentives.

Our analysis uses thousands of customer records extracted from current and historical Telcom databases, with each record detailing customer demographics, tenure, service usage, billing information, and churn status. Beyond the necessary transformations required by certain algorithms, the current state of the data in Telcom’s databases needed minimal manipulation. Descriptive logistic regression and decision tree models developed on all available data were used to learn more about what determines churn behavior. These models revealed the crucial insight that demographics don’t drive churn; customer and service experience do. They indicated that customers with month-to-month plans, electronic check payments, high charges, and subscriptions to internet service were more likely to churn, whereas those with longer tenure, long-term contracts, and engagement with tech support were less likely to churn.

To develop the predictive model that identifies high-risk customers, we refined and tested four different model types: decision trees, random forests, neural networks, and support vector machines (SVMs), each having their respective strengths. The threshold to be determined high- risk is a 25% probability of churn, a level that optimizes sensitivity (correctly identifying churn cases) while not compromising overall accuracy. The models prioritize sensitivity, as the cost of losing recurring customer revenue outweighs that of retention (Kumar, 2022). The decision tree is the predictive model deliverable for this project, as it delivered the highest sensitivity at 79.09% while still maintaining a 75.84% overall accuracy. The balance of sensitivity and interpretability makes it an ideal tool for Telcom’s operational needs.
